🗺️ Six‑Stage Roadmap

Click each stage to explore practical steps, timelines, and teacher‑first principles.

Begin with the school's problems — not with available technology.

Conduct a short audit of:

  • Teacher administrative workload & lesson‑planning time
  • Attendance, behaviour monitoring & assessment processes
  • Data duplication across systems & parent communication
  • Existing digital platforms & subscriptions
  • Areas where teachers repeatedly enter the same information
  • Learning gaps not being identified quickly enough
"Which part of your weekly work takes too much time but adds too little educational value?"

Key action Ask every teacher that question. The answers determine your technology priorities.

Do not attempt digital transformation everywhere at once.

Select one or two high‑impact problems, preferably where technology can simultaneously:

  1. Reduce teacher workload
  2. Improve the quality or speed of information
  3. Enable better instructional decisions
  4. Produce a measurable improvement in student outcomes

📊 Example: Attendance data is collected but follow‑up is slow.

Intervention Automated attendance dashboard

Attendance → Identification of At‑Risk Students → Coordinator Action → Parent Contact → Intervention → Follow‑up

Objective: Better attendance, not a better dashboard.

Select a small group of teachers or one department for a controlled pilot.

Teachers should be involved in:

  • Defining the workflow & testing the tool
  • Identifying unnecessary steps & suggesting improvements
  • Evaluating usability & comparing old and new processes

This protects teacher agency and makes implementation collaborative.

A pilot should answer three questions:
Does it work? • Does it save time? • Does it improve the educational decision?

If no redesign or discontinue it.

Establish a simple baseline before scaling.

Measure four dimensions:

  • Teacher Workload — hours spent on planning/reporting
  • Process Efficiency — time taken to complete a task
  • Instructional Quality — speed/quality of intervention
  • Student Outcomes — attendance, assessment, engagement

⏱️ Example: Before: 45 min preparing a weekly data report. After: 10 min reviewing an auto‑generated report.

Time released: 35 minutes.

What happened to those 35 minutes? If used for student feedback, lesson prep, or collaboration — the technology created educational value.

Once a pilot demonstrates value, expand it carefully.

Establish:

  • One agreed workflow & clear ownership
  • Common data definitions & interoperable systems
  • Shared teacher protocols & short practical training
  • Technical support, data privacy, and safeguarding
  • A mechanism for teacher feedback
"Which existing process or platform can we now simplify, combine or retire?"

This is the difference between digital accumulation and digital transformation.

EdTech implementation should never be considered finished.

At the end of every term, review each major digital tool against five questions:

  1. Is it still solving the original problem?
  2. Is it reducing or increasing teacher workload?
  3. Is it improving teaching or learning?
  4. Is it being used equitably by students and teachers?
  5. Would we purchase it again today?

If consistently no — have the courage to retire the technology.

A mature digital strategy includes both an adoption and a retirement process.

⚖️ The Leadership Rule

For every new digital process introduced, remove, simplify or automate at least one existing process.

➕ New digital processes added 2
➖ Existing processes removed / simplified 1
⚖️ Net: +1 process — you're adding complexity

If a new system requires teachers to enter information twice, check three dashboards, maintain another spreadsheet and attend additional training without eliminating existing work — you have digitised complexity, not transformed.
